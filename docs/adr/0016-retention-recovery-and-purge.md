# Retention, recovery, and purge

Archiving an organisation is reversible for a window and irreversible after it. The archive soft-deletes the organisation, its memberships, its invitations and all of its working simulation data in one transaction, stamping every affected row with the same instant. For `RETENTION_WINDOW_DAYS` (default 30) an owner can undo that through `POST /api/v1/organisations/:organisationId/restore`; once the window closes, the worker's maintenance sweep hard-deletes the working data and the space is reclaimed.

That shared instant is what makes restore correct. Reviving an organisation by clearing every `deletedAt` on its rows would also revive memberships that were removed *before* the archive, quietly handing access back to people who had already lost it. Restore instead matches on the organisation's own `deletedAt` and revives exactly the rows the archive took. Pending invitations are deliberately not revived: the archive revoked them, and an outstanding offer to join should be made again rather than resurrected.

Restore cannot authorise the way every other organisation-scoped route does. `getOrganisationAccess` reads active memberships, and while an organisation is archived it has none, so the usual lookup finds nothing for anybody. The repository proves instead that the caller held owner at the instant of the archive — the one fact that survives it. This is the only route under `{organisationId}` that documents no roles and neither membership `403`, and the contract test names it explicitly so a route that merely forgot its roles still fails.

The purge removes only the replayable synthetic output: intervals, summaries, and the runs that produced them. That is where the volume is, none of it is meter-backed, and every run can be reproduced from its seed, model version and input digest. Three things are never purged. Ledger events are hash-linked evidence about energy and money, and removing one would break the chain that makes the ledger tamper-evident (ADR 0003). Audit events are the record of who did what, including the archive itself. And the organisation and membership rows stay as tombstones, because ledger and audit events reference them — purging those would leave retained evidence pointing at nothing, which is a worse outcome than the bytes they cost.

The sweep runs on the worker's existing maintenance tick, beside the invitation expiry, and is deliberately not transactional. A purge can span a very large number of documents, and a partial pass is harmless because eligibility is derived from the organisation's archive time rather than from how far the last pass got; the next sweep simply finishes. It is also opt-in: a worker started without a retention window does not purge at all, so absent configuration means "do nothing" rather than a default that begins deleting data nobody asked it to. A sweep that finds nothing logs nothing, since finding nothing is the normal case and it runs every interval.
